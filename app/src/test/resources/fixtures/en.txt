The Riverside Library

The small library stood on the bank of a slow river, and every morning the librarian, Dr. Alvarez, unlocked the reading room before the first volunteer arrived. The library was old, but its catalog was new: a volunteer had spent the whole winter typing every title into the digital catalog, and now a reader could find any book in seconds.

Most readers came for the reading room. It was quiet, it was warm, and the windows looked out on the river bank where herons waited for fish. A few readers came only to borrow a book, and the loan desk handled about two hundred loans a week. The catalog showed which copies were on loan and which copies were on the shelf.

Money was a constant worry. Every Friday Dr. Alvarez walked to the bank in the town square to deposit the fines and the small donations. The bank manager knew the library well; she had learned to read in that same reading room thirty years earlier. When the roof began to leak, the bank offered the library a loan with almost no interest, and the town council agreed to repay it.

The volunteers were the heart of the place. One volunteer repaired damaged books, another volunteer ran the children's hour, and a retired teacher taught newcomers how to search the catalog. They were not paid, but the librarian kept a jar of good coffee for them, e.g. the dark roast from the corner shop, and nobody ever complained.

In spring the river rose and flooded the lower bank. Water reached the steps of the library, and the volunteers carried the rare books upstairs one box at a time. The catalog survived because it lived on a server in another town. When the water went down, the reading room smelled of mud for a month, yet the readers returned, the loans continued, and the librarian ordered new shelves.

Nobody in town called it a heritage site. They called it the library, and that was enough.
