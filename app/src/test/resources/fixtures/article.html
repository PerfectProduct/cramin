The Riverside Library

By Town Chronicle staff

The small library stood on the bank of a slow river, and every morning the librarian, Dr. Alvarez, unlocked the reading room before the first volunteer arrived. The library was old, but its catalog was new: a volunteer had spent the whole winter typing every title into the digital catalog, and now a reader could find any book in seconds.

Most readers came for the reading room. It was quiet, it was warm, and the windows looked out on the river bank where herons waited for fish. A few readers came only to borrow a book, and the loan desk handled about two hundred loans a week.

Money and the bank

Money was a constant worry. Every Friday Dr. Alvarez walked to the bank in the town square to deposit the fines and the small donations. The bank manager knew the library well; she had learned to read in that same reading room thirty years earlier.

  • One volunteer repaired damaged books.
  • Another volunteer ran the children's hour.
Nobody in town called it a heritage site. They called it the library, and that was enough.

In spring the river rose and flooded the lower bank. Water reached the steps of the library, and the volunteers carried the rare books upstairs one box at a time. The catalog survived because it lived on a server in another town.
